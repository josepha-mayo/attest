Attest

Tamper-evident visit records for home services, built on the Ring Partner API — what the devices observed, what the worker reported, what a human concluded. Records, not verdicts.

"He said / she said" disputes get resolved from signed evidence instead of memory: every observation, statement, and conclusion is a separate Ed25519-signed receipt on a hash-chained ledger — and the pack you're about to verify carries its own verifier, so nobody has to trust this site either.

The attack gallery — pick one, watch it get caught

One doctored number is the easy case. The verifier defends four different layers, and the attack copies below each hit a different one — the links land directly on the red FAILED stamp:

  • Doctored field — the recorded 12-minute stay inflated to 82: the signed payload_hash no longer matches → payload hash mismatch. zip
  • Deleted record — a visit bundle removed from the zip, but the signed manifest still names its receipt → missing or invalid bundle. zip
  • Smuggled file — extra-evidence.json injected after signing → present but not in the signed manifest. zip
  • Forged verifier — the embedded verify_case.py swapped for an always-green stub → bytes differ from the issuer-signed pin. The pack defends its own checker. zip

Mint your own: attest export --tamper-twin twin.zip --attack missing-record — the twin self-check must refuse it before it ships.

What verification proves — and what it doesn't

A green verdict means

  • Every signature is valid under the pinned issuer key.
  • No record was added, dropped, or altered since signing.
  • The receipts are committed in the signed transparency-log head the pack carries.
  • Redacted media was withheld by policy — its signed digests still match the manifest.

It does not mean

  • That the worker physically attended — camera activity is an observation, not attendance.
  • That absence of events means absence — silence isn't absence.
  • That the issuer is honest — a signature proves integrity under a key, not truth. That's why witness cosigning and the live-log cross-check exist.

What's inside the sample pack

The bundled sample-pack.zip is a real export from the simulated Alvarez residence deployment — media withheld by redaction policy, signed SHA-256 digests preserved. It exercises the hard parts, not just the happy path:

  • a signing-key rotation mid-history: the pack names both issuer keys — the rotated-away key, its signed adoption, and the revocation receipt that retires it;
  • receipts on both sides of the pivot, verified under different keys;
  • a suspect-window annotation — the window signed while the old key was compromised inherits doubt, explicitly;
  • verifier_manifest.json pinning the verifier tooling's own hashes — the pack defends against a hostile verifier shipped inside itself;
  • log_checkpoint.json: a signed RFC 6962-style Merkle tree head with inclusion proofs — the receipts are committed in a transparency log, and the verifier's cross-check can prove the deployment still serves the same head to everyone;
  • monitor observations and a witness cosignature anchored in the witness's own independent log;
  • a split-demo scenario: a deliberate log fork that the witness and monitor jointly catch.
