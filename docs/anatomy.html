What the signature actually covers

The Ed25519 signature signs only the payload's SHA-256. The payload is canonicalized first — fixed key order, fixed float spellings, ASCII timestamps — which is why two implementations produce the same bytes to sign. This is the canonical payload of the receipt above:




The honest boundary. A valid
signature proves these bytes existed under this issuer key at signing time.
It does not prove the camera saw a person, that the worker was
present, that the household told the truth, or that the log you saw is the
only log — the transparency log and witness cosignatures exist to cover those
gaps, and even they can't prove physical presence. Records, not verdicts.



Payload fields mirror the envelope deliberately: the signature
binds the payload; the envelope columns must agree, and any divergence is an
integrity failure, not a reinterpretation. The journal_head field
inside some payloads pins the mutation journal's tip at issuance — a
truncated tail is caught too.
